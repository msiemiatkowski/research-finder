Privacy policy

Last updated 12 November 2026.

Research Finder is a free, open-source website that searches open indexes of studies and data repositories (source code). It runs entirely in your browser: there is no server of ours, no account, no cookies and no analytics or tracking.

Your searches

The keywords you type, and the terms made from them, are sent from your browser directly to the services that answer them: OpenAlex, Wikidata, Wikimedia's machine translation service MinT (which translates the terms into other languages, the titles of results in other languages into English, and an abstract when you choose “English translation”; Settings can turn the translations of results off), DataCite, Zenodo, Harvard Dataverse, OSF (through its search index, SHARE), OpenAIRE, in the tab “Registered studies” ClinicalTrials.gov (the English terms only), and in the tab “Regional databases” LA Referencia, DOAJ and, only with an application ID you add under Settings (kept in your browser and sent only to it), CiNii Research. “Versions of a questionnaire” looks the DOI of a scale's original paper up in OpenAlex. The exports of other databases that you combine with a search (“Remove the duplicates”) are read in your browser and sent nowhere, unless you save the combined records to your Drive. The DOIs of the studies shown, and of the studies you save or export, are sent to Crossref to check whether they were retracted, withdrawn or corrected; for the same check, your browser reads Retraction Watch's list of hijacked journals from Google Sheets (docs.google.com) about once a day, which sends Google nothing about your search (Settings can turn both off). The replications of studies come from a copy of the FORRT Replication Database kept on this site, so looking them up sends nothing anywhere. The DOIs and PubMed IDs of your studies (the ones you add or paste for the review tools) are looked up in OpenAlex. When you ask for a study's reference (“Cite”) or for a reference list, its DOI and the citation style are sent to the DOI Foundation's citation formatter (citation.doi.org). When you save a search, the DOIs of the studies are also sent to Semantic Scholar to find open versions of the papers, figshare and OSF are asked for the lists of files of their datasets, and the open PDFs and data files are downloaded by your browser from where they are published. Their own privacy policies apply to those requests. Research Finder does not see or keep them.

File relay (optional). OSF, figshare and many publishers do not let web pages download their files. If you connect a file relay under Settings, Research Finder sends the addresses of those files (OSF and figshare files, and the open PDFs your browser could not download itself) to that relay, which fetches each file and passes it straight to your browser, keeping nothing. A relay is run by whoever set it up, for example you, on your own free Cloudflare account (its code). Without one, nothing is sent to a relay and those files are listed with their links.

Settings you choose (languages, an OpenAlex key, a file relay, your library's link resolver and proxy, the citation style, whether to show translations and check for retractions), your studies (the list the review tools start from), a copy of Retraction Watch's list of hijacked journals, and the machine translations of your terms and of titles are stored only in your browser (local storage) and can be removed by clearing it. Your library's link resolver and proxy are used only to make the links “Find it at my library” and “Through my library”: when you click one, your browser opens your library's page with the study's details (title, journal, authors, DOI), as any link to it would. An OpenAlex key is sent only to OpenAlex, with your searches. Research Finder no longer asks for an email address; one saved by an earlier version is removed from your browser.

Zotero (optional). If you add a Zotero key under Settings or in “Send to Zotero”, Research Finder sends it only to Zotero, to check it and to add the records you choose (their titles, authors, abstracts, journals, DOIs and keywords) to a new collection in your own Zotero library. The key is kept for this visit only (in the tab's session storage) unless you choose “Remember it in this browser”; you can delete it at any time in your Zotero account.

Google Drive

Connecting Google Drive is optional. If you choose “Connect Google Drive”, you sign in in Google's own window and grant one permission, drive.file: it lets Research Finder create files in your Drive and see or change only the files it created. It cannot see any of your other files.

Research Finder uses this permission only when you click “Save to Drive”, or ask it to run a saved search again or to add files to a save (Settings › Earlier saves): it creates a folder called “Research Finder” and saves there, in one folder per search, the results (a Google Sheet), a list of the files found (a Google Sheet), a document with the details of the search, a file with the search's queries and the identifiers of its results (so that it can be run again), and the open PDFs and data files your browser is allowed to download. Running a search again reads these files and adds the new results to the same folder. The sign-in token stays in your browser's memory for the session (about an hour); it is not stored or sent anywhere except to Google. Research Finder does not collect, store, share or sell any data from your Google account.

You can disconnect under Settings, and remove Research Finder's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Files it saved stay in your Drive until you delete them.

Research Finder's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
