Find the studies and data on your topic in 40 languages, with a record of how you found them

Research Finder adds the other wordings researchers use for your keywords, translates every term into 39 languages and searches about 270 million studies and five open-data repositories. The results, the open PDFs and data files, and the exact queries go into one folder in your own Google Drive.

How Research Finder works

What it does for your research

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses

The whole search, from the first search string to the search appendix

A review is only as complete as its search: a wording or a language the search leaves out is a study the review leaves out. Research Finder does the slow parts of the search for you and shows every step.

  1. Build the search string

    For each keyword it reads the 200 studies that match it best and collects the other wordings they use (for “trust in science”, also “trust in scientists” and “trustworthiness of science”), and adds synonyms from Wikidata. You keep or drop every term.

  2. Search beyond English

    Every term is also searched in 39 other languages, each translation checked by translating it back into English. Studies published in Polish, Spanish or Japanese are found instead of lost to the language bias of an English-only search.

  3. Include the grey literature

    Preprints, theses, datasets and OSF preregistrations are searched next to journal articles, which helps against publication bias.

  4. Run it in your other databases

    Copy the whole search as a Boolean string to adapt for Scopus, Web of Science or PsycINFO, or open it in PubMed, Google Scholar or BASE with one click.

  5. Screen

    Save every record the search finds, with abstracts and without duplicates: a Google Sheet to screen with your co-authors and a RIS file for Rayyan, Covidence, ASReview, Zotero or EndNote.

  6. Collect full texts and data

    Open-access PDFs and open data files go straight into your Drive, each linked from its record: data for effect sizes a paper does not report, or for an individual participant data meta-analysis.

  7. Report and update

    The Search details document records the sources, the date, every term and translation, the filters and the exact queries (PRISMA 2020 items 6 and 7), and the number of records for the flow diagram. Run the queries again when you update the review.

Literature reviews

For a paper, a thesis or a grant proposal

  • Size up a topic: each keyword shows how many studies have it in their title or abstract.
  • Find the existing reviews first with the Review filter.
  • Find the landmark papers and the latest ones: sort by citations or by date, and filter by year, type and open access.
  • Widen the search: related phrases, the keywords of matching studies and the topics most often studied with yours are one click away.
  • Read in one place: the open PDFs in one folder, named author_year_title.

Secondary data analysis

Open datasets to reanalyze, replicate or teach with

  • Five repositories at once: DataCite, Zenodo, Harvard Dataverse, OSF and OpenAIRE.
  • The files themselves: one Drive folder per dataset with its open data files, and a sheet listing every file with its size and download link.
  • Licenses and DOIs in the results sheet, for citing the data you use.
  • Share it with your team: a saved search is an ordinary Drive folder.

Cross-cultural research

What English-only searches miss

  • Adapted measures: validation studies of a questionnaire in another language are often published in national journals, in that language.
  • Local evidence: filter by the language of the study to see what has been published in Polish, Portuguese or Korean.
  • You stay in control: every translation is shown, and any term or language can be switched off.

How a search is built

A real example: the keyword “trust in science”, searched in November 2026. While you search, the page shows every step, and you can change any of them.

  1. You type

    trust in science

    Found in the 200 studies that match best (of 2,378 in OpenAlex), and searched too:

    Offered to add: related phrases and the keywords of those studies:

    public trustepistemic trustscience communicationpublic understanding of scienceresearch integritymisinformation

    175 translations, each translated back into English as a check:

    Five came back with another meaning and were left out, such as Czech “věda” (just “science”).

  4. The search

    One Boolean query (OR within a keyword, AND between keywords) in the titles and abstracts of OpenAlex, and in the five data repositories. A query longer than a service takes is split into several, English terms first:

    ("trust in science" OR "trust in scientists" OR "trustworthiness of science" OR "trusted science" OR "trust science" OR "science trustworthy" OR "scientific trust")
("confianza en la ciencia" OR "confiance dans la science" OR "Vertrauen in die Wissenschaft" OR "confiança na ciência" OR "对科学的信任" OR "доверие к науке" OR "科学への信頼" OR "zaufanie do nauki" OR …)

Where it searches

Open scholarly databases, asked directly from your browser. No subscription is needed.

OpenAlex
About 270 million works from every field: articles, preprints, books, theses and datasets. Titles and abstracts, or full texts too.
DataCite
Datasets with DOIs from research repositories around the world.
Zenodo
CERN’s open repository. Data files are copied into your Drive.
Harvard Dataverse
One of the largest open repositories of research data. Data files are copied into your Drive.
OSF
Projects and preregistrations from the Open Science Framework.
OpenAIRE
Datasets in the European open science graph.

Also used: Wikidata for synonyms and names in other languages, Wikimedia MinT for machine translation, and Semantic Scholar for open versions of papers, such as arXiv preprints.

Good to know

  • Use it with your bibliographic databases. OpenAlex is broad, but a systematic review usually also searches databases such as PsycINFO, MEDLINE, Scopus or Web of Science. The copied search string gives you a head start there.
  • Check the translations. They are machine translations, each checked by translating it back. Look through them before relying on them, and switch off any that do not fit.
  • Titles and abstracts by default. A study without an abstract in OpenAlex is found only by its title. Searching full texts too finds more, less precisely.
  • A free daily allowance. Without a key, OpenAlex allows about 100 requests a day per network, each bringing up to 200 results, and reaches only the first 10,000 results of a search. A free personal key, added under Settings, allows ten times more and reaches every result.
  • Some files stay as links. Many publishers do not let browsers download their PDFs, and neither do OSF and figshare. Those are listed with their links. A file relay you set up under Settings (free, on Cloudflare) copies the OSF and figshare files and fetches many of those PDFs too; publishers that let only browsers in keep theirs as links.
